J. TREATMENT & DISINFECTION TECHNOLOGIES
Topic: Autoclave vs microwave for biomedical waste disinfection

Finding: Review compares microwave and autoclave treatment; notes traditional methods include incineration (pyrolysis, rotary kiln, plasma), chemical disinfection, and high-temperature steam (autoclave); flags high investment/technical needs. Suggests autoclave + shredder may be a better alternative for hazardous healthcare waste in many cases; landfilling ranked lowest.
Source: PMC / Academia.edu
URLs: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9045020/ ; https://www.academia.edu/99582119/...
Why relevant: Establishes the treatment-technology options and trade-offs your on-board or downstream disinfection would sit among.
Limitations: Read full review for the specific assessment criteria. "Better alternative" is context-dependent.
Topic: Autoclave operating parameters

Finding: Autoclave commonly operates with steam at 121°C, minimum ~30 minutes, at ≥15 psi (per a secondary article); ScienceDirect topic page describes microwave treatment (shred → humidify → microwave → steam disinfection).
Sources: The Perfect Voice (secondary blog, Sep 2025); ScienceDirect Topics
URLs: https://www.theperfectvoice.in/post/from-microwaves-to-autoclaves-... ; https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/earth-and-planetary-sciences/autoclaving
Why relevant: Baseline operating parameters if you discuss on-board or integrated autoclaving.
Limitations: 121°C/30 min/15 psi is a widely repeated figure but from a secondary source here — verify against a standard/primary source (e.g., WHO Blue Book or CPCB). Confirm applicability to specific waste categories.
Topic: Autoclave limitation — bulky/anatomical waste

Finding: A comparative study notes that an autoclave without a shredder cannot treat bulky waste such as anatomical waste.
Source: PMC (Isfahan hospitals comparative study, 2010 data)
URL: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3633376/
Why relevant: Identifies a real limitation — relevant if your system claims to handle all categories.
Limitations: Older study (2010 data), single-region. Anatomical waste (yellow) typically requires incineration, not autoclave — verify against BMW Rules treatment-per-category.
Topic: Commercial integrated microwave/autoclave + shredder systems

Finding: Vendors sell integrated medical-waste microwave/autoclave units with built-in shredders. One vendor (Aucma) claims microwave disinfection achieving 99.9999% kill rate of Bacillus subtilis var. niger spores (ATCC 9372) in 30–45 min (up to 99.999999%). Sinicmed and LiYing describe combined microwave+steam systems with shredders and capacity tables.
Sources: Aucma; LiYing; Sinicmed (vendor pages)
URLs: https://www.aucmamedicalglobal.com/Medical-Waste-Microwaves-autoclaves-with-integrated-shredder-pd594702268.html ; https://www.liyingtec.com/microwave-medical-waste-solutions/ ; https://www.sinicmed.com/medical-waste-treatment-technology-specifications-and-requirements-1/
Why relevant: Shows commercially available integrated shred+disinfect technology — relevant to your packing/treatment ambitions and to "what already exists commercially."
Limitations: Vendor marketing claims — kill-rate and capacity figures are unverified manufacturer statements. These are stationary industrial units, not mobile. Do not present kill-rate claims as established fact.

Topic: IoT modernization review (RFID/QR feasibility)

Source: MDPI Sustainability
URL: https://www.mdpi.com/2071-1050/15/13/9909
Date: Jun 21, 2023
